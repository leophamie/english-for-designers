# How I made Letterform T

## Process

1. I started by looking at images of Gothic type. I wanted my letter to be ornamental and decorative, but still easy to read.

2. I tried to add my own twist to the Gothic style. Instead of building the T from just two straight lines, I used a wavy top stroke, a long curve that sweeps around to the left, and a thin hairline on the right.

3. The final T looks very different from a T drawn the traditional way, but it still reads as the letter T. That balance between decoration and legibility is what I like most about it.

## Haiku drafts

I started with the curve of the T, which reminded me of a crescent moon. My first tries at the opening line were too short (4 syllables):

- Round like the moon
- Curves of a moon

I added one more syllable to get "Curvy like a moon," and then wrote about the sharp Gothic strokes and the thin line on the right, which looks like a harp string.

### Final

Curvy like a moon,  
a form sharp yet elegant,  
one string makes a harp

## Other ways to describe the Letterform

- **Focus on form:** A letter T made of three parts: a wavy, blade-like top bar, a long stroke that curves down and to the left like a C, and a thin straight line on the right.
- **Focus on mood:** A dark, elegant T that feels old and ornamental, like a letter from a Gothic manuscript, but with a modern, sharp twist.
